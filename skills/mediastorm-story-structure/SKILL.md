# MediaStorm Story Structure

Build the edit around information, viewer attention, and relationships between shots rather than around effects or command names.

## Core workflow

1. Identify the viewer promise, central question, subject relationships, and what the ending must prove.
2. Map material before ordering it: people, events, locations, themes, repetitions, contradictions, and useful visual/auditory echoes.
3. Choose an organizing frame for the material: issue-driven explanation, spatial progression, time reconstruction, comparison, character/event flow, or another frame supported by the footage.
4. Give each section a job: establish context, add information, change state, create contrast, answer a promise, or release tension. Remove or compress sections that do no job.
5. Evaluate shot length against information density and emotional contrast, not a universal seconds-per-shot rule.
6. Use matching shape, position, movement, sound, or idea to justify montage transitions. Check whether juxtaposition accidentally creates a false causal claim.
7. Review the full structure for promise fulfillment, alternation between intensity and relief, and whether B-roll clarifies rather than merely decorates.

## Boundaries

- Treat course timing examples, opening lengths, channel duration preferences, and “clock” positions as diagnostic examples, not universal rules.
- Do not let automatic beat markers or scene cuts replace editorial judgment.
- Do not infer that every project needs music or transitions; information progression can justify restraint.
- The 2-second picture pass verified specific cut lengths, price ladder, opening blocks, and mind-map branches; clock positions remain a diagnostic, and course 15's own clock pointer conflicts with its 3-point card.
- Treat this skill as provisional until continuous course audio/video review is completed.
